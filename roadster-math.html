The math behind "0.55 s? Does it add up?" (full sheet)

Every input is either claimed by Tesla (T), reported by one outlet (R) or my assumption (A). Everything else is arithmetic you can redo with a calculator. Script: calc.py (Python, no libraries; run python3 calc.py).

Constants: g = 9.81 m/s². 1 mph = 0.44704 m/s, so 60 mph = 26.82 m/s. 1 atm = 101,325 Pa.

1. What 0–60 in t seconds demands

Average acceleration a = 26.82 / t.

0–60 timea (m/s²)in g
1.00 s (recorded at the event)26.82.73
0.81 s (live timer)33.13.38
0.67 s (Tesla reply, "rollout subtracted")40.04.08
0.59 s (Rocket spec)45.54.63
0.55 s (interview claim)48.84.97

Average only; the peak at the start is higher. Rollout (the first foot or so that timing rigs ignore) is not modelled.

2. Tyres alone (A: μ = 1.2 to 1.6)

Friction limit a_max = μ g (no downforce). All wheels driven: still μ g.

μ = 1.2 → 1.2 g. μ = 1.6 → 1.6 g. So 2.7 g or more needs something that is not tyre friction (downforce or thrust).

3. Thrust alone (T: >80 kN; mass not published, R: ~1,700 kg, A: ~2,350 kg)

a = F / m.

1,700 kg: 80,000 / 1,700 = 47.1 m/s² = 4.8 g. 2,350 kg: 34.0 m/s² = 3.5 g.

4. Motor power (T: >1 MW)

F = P / v. At 10 mph: 223.7 kN. At 30 mph: 74.6 kN. At 60 mph: 37.3 kN. At low speed the limit is tyre grip, not power.

5. Does the gas last? (T: 600 L, R: ~250 kg of gas; A: nitrogen at room temperature)

Mass flow = thrust / exhaust speed: ṁ = F / v_e.

Cold nitrogen exit speed has a physical upper bound of about 780 m/s (full expansion), realistically 600–700.

At v_e = 700 m/s: ṁ = 80,000 / 700 = 114 kg/s. 250 kg / 114 kg/s = 2.2 s.

At 600 m/s: 1.9 s. At 780 m/s: 2.4 s.

Reported burn is ~5–6 s. To burn 250 kg in 6 s: ṁ = 42 kg/s, and 80 kN would need v_e = 1,920 m/s, which cold gas can't do. So either 80 kN is a peak (average at 700 m/s over 6 s = 29 kN), or the burn is pulsed, or the figures differ. That is the open question.

6. Fans and downforce (A: sealed underbody area 2.0 m²)

Downforce D = Δp × A. To hold the car upside down: D ≥ m g.

1,700 kg: 16.7 kN → Δp = 8.3 kPa (8.2% of an atmosphere). 2,350 kg: 23.1 kN → 11.5 kPa (11.4%).

Hard ceiling: Δp can't exceed 1 atm, so D ≤ 203 kN at 2 m².

Extra grip from downforce at μ = 1.5: 1,000 kgf → 14.7 kN; 2,000 kgf → 29.4 kN; 4,700 kgf → 69.2 kN.

(The 4,700 on the demo HUD is a gauge reading, not a published spec.)

7. Stored gas energy (R: 10,000 psi; T: 600 L)

Isothermal E ≈ p V ln(p/p0) = 690e5 × 0.6 × ln(690e5/101325) ≈ 270 MJ (75 kWh). Over 6 s that is 45 MW of gas power, against about 28 MW of jet power (80 kN × 700 m/s / 2). Plausible order of magnitude; not a proof of anything.

8. Hover and turning (A: lever arm 1 m, thruster 2 kN, inertia I = m k², k = 1.2 m)

Hover needs T ≥ m g: 16.7 kN (1,700 kg) or 23.1 kN (2,350 kg), i.e. 21–29% of 80 kN. Gas at 700 m/s: 23.8–32.9 kg/s, so 250 kg lasts 7.6–10.5 s of pure hover.

Turn: τ = r F = 1 m × 2 kN = 2 kN·m; I = 2,000 × 1.2² = 2,880 kg·m²; α = τ / I = 0.69 rad/s²; a half-turn from rest takes sqrt(2π / α) = 3.0 s.

What would settle it (from the video)

1. An independent timed run with the rollout rule stated. 2. The car's weight. 3. Tank pressure and the thrust curve over time. 4. A real downforce number, not just an upside-down claim.

All numbers are from the 15 Oct 2026 reveal coverage (Electrek, CNBC, TechCrunch, Teslarati, InsideEVs). I'm checking arithmetic, not accusing anyone.
